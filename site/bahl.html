How it started

One pull up. One very obvious silhouette.

JB31 weighs 196 pounds. He strapped 140 pounds of kettlebells to a belt and pulled his chin over the bar. Everybody watching saw the same thing at the same time: two giant iron balls swinging between his legs.

A formula followed. Then a name. Then rules, tiers, a board and a whole family of lifts. Ed Zercher got one squat named after him. Bahl gets a dynasty.

Bahl Pull
noun
A pull up with iron hung from a belt, kettlebells or the Bahl Sack, starting on the floor. One chin hair on the bar counts. The test behind the index.
Bahl Squat
noun
A belt squat with dumbbells hanging from the belt. Named after the man, the same way the Zercher squat was.
noun · on the drawing board
The rig behind the Bahl Squat. A hip belt, a strap and a steel yoke that hangs two dumbbells side by side.
Bahl Drop
noun
How low your bells hang. Set it before every set. "What's your Bahl Drop?"

Official rules

How a Bahl Pull counts

  1. Weigh in the same day. Bodyweight is what the scale says, not what you remember.
  2. Iron hangs from a belt: kettlebells or a loaded Bahl Sack. It starts on the floor with the chain just tight.
  3. Start from a dead hang with straight arms. No pinning the iron with your feet.
  4. One chin hair touching the bar counts. One clean rep is a score.
  5. Video or two witnesses, or it did not happen.
  6. Protests go to the Commissioner. His rulings default to no.

Locker room questions

Asked and answered

Why 31?
Because JB31 said so. Also, hanging your exact bodyweight comes out to a perfect 31.
Kilograms?
Fine. Use the same unit for both numbers and the math still works.
What if I hang more than my bodyweight?
Then you are Wrecking Balls. Next level. The Commissioner will want video.
Can I protest a score?
You can. It will be no.
